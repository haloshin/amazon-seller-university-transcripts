# Address a Multiple Account Policy violation

Welcome to the video on addressing Amazon's multiple accounts policy
 violations. This video covers the following topics; 1. What can you do to
 address violations? 2. Best practices to remain compliant. The content in this
 video applies to all stores. Let us first understand how you can respond to a
 violation notice. If you receive a notification that a related account has
 been deactivated, this means that one of your existing accounts violated Amazon's
 policies. In order to reactivate your related account, you must first address
 the violation in the underlying account that was responsible for the policy
 violations by submitting an appeal for each violation. You can appeal by signing
 into the account that was responsible for the policy violations. You must appeal
 in each regional Amazon store in which the account has been deactivated for the
 policy violation. You can submit your appeal by accessing your account health
 dashboard. If your appeal for the underlying account that was responsible
 for the policy violations is accepted and the account is successfully reactivated,
 you can then appeal your related account. When submitting the appeal, you will need
 to provide us with the storefront name of the original account that was reactivated
 and the date of reactivation. Use case number one. You own two selling accounts,
 seller storefront one, and seller storefront two. Seller storefront one
 receives a deactivation notice for directing customers to an external site
 circumventing the sales process. You then receive a deactivation notice on
 seller storefront two since it is related to seller storefront one. You should log
 into your seller storefront one account and appeal the violation. If your appeal
 is accepted and the seller storefront one account is successfully reactivated,
 then log into your seller storefront two account and appeal through the
 account health dashboard by providing the storefront name of the original
 account that was reactivated and the date of reactivation. If you believe the
 related account deactivation happened in error or you have never owned or
 operated another account, please submit an appeal through the account health
 dashboard and confirm that the account is not related to yours. If we do not
 receive evidence confirming the account is not related to you, the account will
 not be reinstated. If you previously co-owned or co-operated the underlying
 account with a former business partner, you would need to provide supporting
 documentation such as a contract showing separation of the business relationship
 as part of the appeal. Please note that it's against policy to buy, sell, or
 transfer your account to or from another seller. Let us learn about the best
 practices to remain compliant. Sellers who have a legitimate business reason for
 operating more than one account and maintain all accounts in good standing
 will not be at risk for a multiple accounts deactivation. Please make sure
 you only create additional accounts if there is a valid business reason. Examples
 of valid business reasons include owning separate brands with different
 storefront names or selling products manufactured by different companies. When
 you receive notices or see alerts on your account health dashboard or
 performance notifications from Amazon that indicate you are violating Amazon
 selling policies, please correct the behavior and submit an appeal if applicable
 as soon as possible. Opening a new account instead of addressing the
 underlying violations within your existing accounts is a violation of
 Amazon's policies and will only trigger an automatic deactivation on your
 related accounts. All sellers are responsible for understanding and
 adhering to the terms and conditions set out by Amazon's business solutions
 agreement which is part of the registration process and includes the
 selling policies and seller code of conduct. All sellers are expected to
 maintain and keep secure their own account information and credentials. In
 the primary account information, sellers should not list information of any
 third-party services they may be using to help manage the account. However, if
 you have engaged a valid third-party service, we recommend listing them as a
 secondary user in the approved app Users User Permissions. We hope this video on
 our multiple accounts policy was helpful. Thank you and happy selling on Amazon!
