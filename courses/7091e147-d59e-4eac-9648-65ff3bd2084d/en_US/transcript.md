# Help with Battery Questions

Batteries are highly regulated. To ensure they are properly categorized, we need to collect specific
 details about the battery. When selling a battery or a product with a battery, you will be asked
 a few additional questions. You are required to provide accurate information. Missing or
 inaccurate information can lead to safety incidents, delays in setting up the product
 and getting it to customers quickly, and regulatory action. Let's get started. What
 is the composition of the battery you are selling? Common composition types are Lithium
 ion, Lithium metal, alkaline, and nickel metal hydride. Most of the time, the composition
 will be printed on the battery. Or, it can also be found in the manufacturer's documentation.
 If you are selling a Lithium battery, we will ask you a few more questions. How many
 batteries are included in your listing? The number of batteries is a critical piece of
 information for us. Let's take a look at a listing for a set of walkie-talkie radios.
 At first glance, you may think of each radio using one battery. Really, the complete listing
 contains two batteries, so your listing should specify two batteries. You will also be asked
 about the number of cells. Battery regulations make a distinction between a single cell battery
 and a multi-cell battery. Common examples of single cell batteries would be the double
 A batteries used in a flashlight, or the coin cell found in your watch. A common example
 of a multi-cell battery is the battery in your laptop. Laptop batteries can have up
 to nine Lithium ion cells linked together within the plastic battery casing to provide
 longer battery life. The number of cells for multi-cell batteries can typically be found
 on the manufacturer's website or in their product documentation. What is the weight
 of the battery or batteries? This refers to the total weight of all Lithium batteries
 minus the product. Batteries can be weighed manually, but this should also be available
 in the manufacturer's documentation. What is the battery type? Common battery types
 are double A, triple A, C-R-1-2-3-A, C-R-2, and 9 volt. The battery type is usually printed
 on the battery, or it can be found in the manufacturer's documentation. We will also
 ask you how the battery is packaged. There are three ways batteries are typically shipped.
 Batteries only, batteries contained in equipment, and batteries packed with equipment. If you
 are selling stand-alone batteries, select batteries only. For example, a spare laptop
 battery, or an external battery charger like a power bank, or if you are including other
 products not used by the battery like a bonus charging cord, even a power bank integrated
 into a product like a purse for convenient charging is considered batteries only when
 answering this question. Select batteries contained in equipment if you are selling a
 battery that is pre-installed in the product or not removable. For example, tablets, solar
 lights, wireless speakers, LED candles, and wireless headphones. Though you can't see
 the battery, these products do contain batteries and are categorized as batteries contained
 in equipment. Select batteries packed with equipment if your listing includes a battery
 shipped with the product and it is not pre-installed. Everyday items that commonly come
 with a battery packed alongside it are power tools, flashlights, and digital cameras. Next,
 you will be asked to provide the watt hour rating. Battery sales are regulated and restricted
 based on the watt hour rating. The maximum allowable watt hour rating can vary by country,
 so check Seller Central to ensure that your battery complies with the battery selling
 policy for your marketplace. You can find the watt hours printed on the battery or on
 the battery pack. If the watt hours are not printed on the battery and the manufacturer's
 documentation is not available, you can calculate watt hours yourself using this formula. Milliamp hours divided by 1000 times the voltage. Check Seller Central Help for more information.
 Lithium metal battery sales are also regulated and restricted based on the grams of lithium
 contained in the battery. For this information, check the manufacturer's documentation. If
 the lithium content is not available in the documentation, then it can be calculated using
 this formula. Milliamp hours divided by 1000 times 0.3 to give you the lithium content in
 grams. Need more help answering these questions? Go to help in Seller Central and videos on
 Seller University. Both are great resources for learning more. Thank you for helping to
 keep our communities and our customers safe.
